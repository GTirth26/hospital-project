Anxiety Disorders

Anxiety is a common mental health disorder characterized by persistent feelings of worry, nervousness, and fear. While occasional anxiety is a normal part of life, anxiety disorders involve more than temporary worry or fear.

Types of Anxiety Disorders

  • Generalized Anxiety (GAD): Excessive worry about daily life.
  • Panic Disorder: Frequent, sudden episodes of intense fear.
  • Social Anxiety: Intense fear of social interactions.
  • Specific Phobias: Irrational fear of specific objects or situations.
  • OCD: Recurrent, unwanted thoughts and repetitive behaviors.

Causes and Risk Factors

  • Genetics: Family history can increase susceptibility.
  • Brain Chemistry: Imbalance in neurotransmitter levels.
  • Environmental Stress: Traumatic life events or chronic stress.
  • Medical Factors: Underlying health conditions.

Dr. Edna B. Foa

Professor of Clinical Psychology, University of Pennsylvania

Dr. Edna Foa is a world-renowned clinical psychologist and a leading expert in the treatment of anxiety disorders, particularly PTSD and OCD. She pioneered Prolonged Exposure therapy, which has become a gold standard for treating trauma.

Key Contributions:

  • Developed Exposure and Response Prevention (ERP) for OCD.
  • Pioneered Prolonged Exposure (PE) therapy for PTSD.
  • Published over 20 books and hundreds of research articles.
  • Recipient of numerous prestigious psychology awards.
